How XEROrank works

Enter one or more medication names. XEROrank resolves each name to a standard RxNorm identifier through the National Library of Medicine's RxNav service, retrieves the current FDA label from openFDA, and assigns a xerogenic-potential score from 0 (no signal) to 3 (strong signal) using the XEROrank classification framework. The scores roll up into a regimen tier — low, moderate or high — and the app drafts a chart note that you can copy into your record system.

Who it is for

Licensed healthcare professionals: dentists, dental hygienists, physicians, nurse practitioners, physician assistants and pharmacists. XEROrank is a reference tool. It does not diagnose xerostomia, and decisions to start, stop or change any medication rest with the treating clinician and the prescriber.

Common questions

A medication is not found. Try the generic name (for example, "oxybutynin" rather than a brand name), check the spelling, and make sure the device is online — labels are retrieved live.

The label excerpt looks different from the package insert I have. openFDA serves the most recent labeling submitted by the manufacturer; older printed inserts may differ. The RxNorm identifier shown lets you confirm the exact product matched.

A score seems too high or too low. The score reflects the label's adverse-reaction language and the framework's rules, not an individual patient's experience. Use your clinical judgment; if you think the rule itself is wrong for a drug, email us with the drug name and the label text you are looking at.

Does the app store patient information? No. See the privacy policy.

Data sources

openFDA drug labeling (U.S. Food and Drug Administration) and RxNav / RxNorm (U.S. National Library of Medicine). Both are public U.S. Government data services; XEROrank is not affiliated with or endorsed by either agency.
